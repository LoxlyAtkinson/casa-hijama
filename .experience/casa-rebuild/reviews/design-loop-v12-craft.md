# Design Loop craft review — v12

**Verdict: FAIL.** The biggest gap is the live body's visual finish. The fallback still in `mobile-body-fallback.png` and `mobile-body-fallback-back.png` has luminous edges, fine internal structure and a localized warm focus. The live figure in `desktop-body-front.png`, `desktop-body-upper.png`, `desktop-body-lower-zoom.png` and their mobile counterparts is more opaque and smooth, with sparse grid lines. At 200% it becomes a large, featureless cyan torso. Beside the clinical reference, the live result reads closer to a translucent mannequin than a scan. It remains synthetic and generally comfortable, but has not reached the requested brighter anatomical quality of its own still.

## Rendered observations

1. **Live-to-still mismatch (bar 5–7; C23, C29):** The still's fine structure and white-cyan edge light disappear when WebGL takes over. The live face and torso are softly molded with little inner detail. The active coral focus is present, but its small flat label/dot does not add the spatial detail suggested by the reference. A feasible repair is to strengthen rim lighting and add restrained, depth-following anatomical linework or layered translucency on the live surface. Keep one localized focus and avoid realistic organs or a diagnostic-looking map.
2. **Zoom framing (bar 2, 7; C22–24):** At 200%, `desktop-body-neck-zoom.png` and `desktop-body-lower-zoom.png` enlarge the same sparse texture while cutting arms and lower body at hard frame edges. `mobile-body-neck-zoom.png` and `mobile-body-lower-zoom.png` put a much larger cropped torso directly above the chapter selector. The selected point is visible, but the closer view has no corresponding increase in visual information. An area-specific close framing with finer surface detail and a soft edge/fade would make the zoom feel intentional.
3. **Mobile hierarchy:** In `mobile-body-front.png` the body, zoom rail, turn control, chapter selector, description and CTA all occupy one 844px frame. The CTA overlays the last lines of the description in the capture. Give the text and CTA a nonoverlapping reading area; the model can be slightly shorter or the CTA can follow the paragraph in normal flow. Check all three mobile chapters and zoom states at the same viewport.
4. **Hero comparison (bar 1–4; C22):** `desktop-hero-start.png` makes a single cup dominant with a concise headline and clear booking action. `desktop-hero-room.png` changes the subject materially and gives the room a distinct warm scene. The mobile crops preserve these subjects and the booking route. These panels are closer to the Apple pacing bar than the body section, whose persistent side columns and small model change less in composition across the three chapter captures. A chapter-specific camera frame or stronger subject scale shift would make the body sequence more editorial without hiding controls.

The static captures show distinct front/back chapters, warm active markers, zoom states, and a visibly different drag angle. Reduced-motion and fallback captures retain a readable figure and controls. Still PNGs cannot prove animation smoothness, timing, full-turn continuity, click/keyboard behavior, or that a WebGL transition avoids a flash; those require live browser observation. I did not inspect implementation code, builder rationale, or prior reviews. The shared filesystem means this reviewer cannot establish runtime or artifact isolation from the builder; this is independent visual judgment of the supplied v6 PNGs, not a claim of process blinding.
